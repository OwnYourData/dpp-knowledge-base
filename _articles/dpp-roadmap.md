<div class="tldr" markdown="1">
<p class="label">In short</p>

- **Batteries are first:** electric-vehicle, light-means-of-transport and larger industrial batteries need a passport from **18 February 2027**.
- Under the Ecodesign Regulation (ESPR), each product group gets its own **delegated act**. **Iron and steel** is next; the Commission expects to adopt it in Q4 2026. Textiles, tyres and aluminium are planned for 2027, furniture for 2028, mattresses for 2029.
- A delegated act applies **at the earliest 18 months** after it enters into force. Passports for these groups therefore start in 2028 at the earliest.
- Toys, detergents and construction products get passports under their own regulations.
- As of mid-October 2026, no ESPR delegated act with passport requirements has been adopted.
</div>

## How a passport becomes mandatory

The ESPR, in force since July 2024, creates the framework: it says *that* products will have passports and how the system works. It does not say *which* products need one or *what* goes into it. That is decided product group by product group in delegated acts.

Iron and steel has passed the consultation; the other planned groups are in the preparatory study." caption="From working plan to obligation. The callouts show where the product groups stood in October 2026." width="960" height="330" %}

Two numbers in the regulation set the pace:

- Parliament and Council have **two months**, extendable by two, to object before a delegated act enters into force.
- Its date of application may not be earlier than **18 months** after entry into force, except in duly justified cases. Every product group therefore gets at least a year and a half of lead time.

The order comes from the Commission's **Working Plan 2025–2030**, adopted on 16 April 2025. Its dates are indicative and will be reviewed in 2028.

"Planned" means the year in which the Working Plan expects the Commission to adopt the delegated act – not the year from which passports are required.

## Batteries: the first passport

From 18 February 2027, every electric-vehicle battery, every battery for light means of transport such as e-bikes and e-scooters, and every industrial battery above 2 kWh placed on the EU market needs a passport. The obligation lies with whoever places the finished battery on the market. Simplification packages adopted or proposed so far leave this date unchanged.

What is still missing are the Commission's rules on who counts as a person with a **legitimate interest** and what they may access. The regulation required them by 18 August 2026; that deadline passed. The public part and the part for authorities can already be built against Annex XIII of the regulation. How the access groups work in practice is described in [Role-based access to passport data]({{ '/role-based-access/' | relative_url }}).

## ESPR product groups: one after another

**Iron and steel** is the first ESPR product group. The Commission published a call for evidence and a public consultation in May 2026, open until 12 August 2026. The measures under consideration include information on the carbon footprint and recycled content, substances that hinder recycling, performance classes based on the carbon footprint, and a passport for all actors in the supply chain.

**Textiles and apparel, tyres and aluminium** are planned for 2027, **furniture** for 2028 and **mattresses** for 2029. For each, the Joint Research Centre is preparing the technical basis in a preparatory study with stakeholder meetings.

Alongside the product groups, the Working Plan foresees two **horizontal measures**: a repairability score, planned for 2027, and requirements on recycled content and recyclability of electrical and electronic equipment, planned for 2029.

Some products are not in this first plan. **Footwear** gets its own study, to be completed by the end of 2027; for **chemicals**, a study is to define the scope first. Both will be reassessed in the mid-term review in 2028.

**Energy-related products** that already have ecodesign rules – dishwashers, fridges, displays and others – get updated rules under the ESPR between 2026 and 2030. Where a product carries an energy label, the existing EPREL database may take the place of a separate passport.

## Other regulations with passports

- **Toys:** the new Toy Safety Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 requires a passport for toys and applies from 1 August 2030.
- **Detergents:** the new Detergents Regulation (EU) 2026/405 introduces a passport for detergents; it applies from 23 September 2029.
- **Construction products:** under the Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110, passports follow product family by product family, through delegated acts and the standards they reference.

## What this means today

The **content** of most passports is still open. The **infrastructure** is not: the EU registry has been live since 20 July 2026, and six harmonised standards for identifiers, data carriers, storage, APIs and the data model were published in 2026. A passport built on those standards today will not have to be rebuilt when your product group is due – only filled with the data its delegated act requires.

> This roadmap reflects the state of 16 October 2026. Dates in the Working Plan are indicative, and drafts change during consultation. Check the linked sources before relying on a date.
